Medical Device Redesign for Patient Care

+

EMPOWER Engineering Club / Edwards Lifesciences

Redesigned a neonatal CPAP mask to improve airflow, comfort, and attachment reliability for preterm infants in the NICU.

Outcome

Reduced turbulent flow by 36% (FEA); project extended by the EMPOWER board with additional funding for validation and patent filing.

Problem

The existing NICU CPAP mask was bulky, unstable, and prone to turbulent flow — reducing respiratory efficiency and causing skin breakdown in premature infants. Poor fit and limited facial access hindered both comfort and clinical usability.

My Role

As Design Lead, I led all CAD modeling and design iterations of the CPAP interface, incorporating clinical feedback and engineering constraints to improve flow dynamics and patient usability.

Outcome

  • Reduced turbulent flow by 36.05% (FEA), promoting more consistent pressure delivery
  • Over 60% reduction in rotational inertia across all axes
  • Passed leakage testing under NICU conditions
  • Project extended by the EMPOWER board — funding for validation, manufacturing, sterilization, and patent filing

Regenerative Medicine Research

+

CU Anschutz Medical Campus / UC Health

Supported development of a mechanical stretch assay to study platelet-rich-plasma (PRP) effects on tendon progenitor cells from chronic tendinopathy patients.

Outcome

Established a working in vitro testing platform; findings presented at the 6th Annual Orthopedic Research Symposium.

Problem

Chronic tendinopathy remains difficult to treat due to poor healing and limited regenerative options. PRP is a promising biologic therapy, but clinical outcomes are inconsistent due to variability in preparation and a lack of standardized testing platforms.

My Role

Organized and digitized the human tendon tissue biobank, contributed to the IRB submission and project proposal, and participated in biweekly research meetings. Performed TPC plating, microscopy-based morphology analysis, and data logging to evaluate regenerative effects of PRP formulations. Helped draft and finalize the research abstract.

Outcome

Established a working in vitro platform to evaluate PRP effects on chronic tendon cells under mechanical load. Findings supported an IRB submission and were presented at the 6th Annual Orthopedic Research Symposium. Ongoing work includes extended PRP testing and gene expression analysis.

Land & Resource Analysis for Development

+

J&T Consulting

Performed detailed land analysis for a private land holdings client — survey data, subsurface modeling, and reservoir capacity estimates across 13 tracts.

Outcome

Identified additional minable surface beyond initial estimates; tracts are now scheduled as long-term water reservoir sites.

Problem

Existing site models relied on outdated data, limiting accuracy for resource planning. The client needed a full redesign to maximize profitability while meeting regulatory compliance.

Process

  • Survey points & data collection across multiple tracts
  • Borings & depth logs to distinguish surface, clay, and bedrock layers
  • 3D terrain surface creation from survey and boring data
  • Profiles & alignments to evaluate elevation changes and constraints
  • Grading & volume calculations for cut-and-fill operations
  • Stage-storage & reservoir capacity estimates

Outcome

Discovered over 4 million square yards of additional minable surface beyond initial estimates. All tracts are now scheduled to become long-term water reservoir sites after completion.

Inclusive Product Design

+

Empower Engineering Club / Callaway Golf

Designed an adaptive golf glove for players with limited grip strength, in collaboration with Callaway — restoring swing control without interfering with mechanics.

Outcome

Placed 2nd overall in Cal Poly's campus-wide design competition.

Problem

Traditional golf gloves offer minimal support for players with hand weakness or impaired grip. Without a secure connection to the club, adaptive athletes struggle to maintain control, resulting in inconsistent shots and increased injury risk.

The Build

Embedded a BOA cable-and-dial closure system (the Li2 and IP1 dials, selected for compact form and reliability) for one-handed operation, plus a custom bag-mounted attachment that lets the dial be tightened using only one hand — similar to buckling a seatbelt.

Market

With over 25 million golfers in the US — many over age 50 — grip strength issues are common. Total manufacturing cost landed between $30–40 per unit, positioning the glove as a scalable, affordable option in the adaptive golf equipment market.
